Financial and Credit Indicators

The financial side of the US economy since 1950: how stocks are priced, how much households, businesses and governments borrow, how banks lend, and how tight financial conditions are. Each page shows the series in levels and their cyclical components, with business-cycle statistics against output, as on the Real Business Cycles page.

Pages

Stock Market

The S&P 500 from Robert J. Shiller’s data: real prices, dividends and earnings, the price–dividend, price–earnings and cyclically adjusted (CAPE) ratios, returns and the excess return over Treasury bills.

Debt and Leverage

Debt of households, nonfinancial businesses and governments, in real terms and relative to GDP, corporate leverage, credit to the private sector and the Basel credit-to-GDP gap.

Banks and Credit Supply

Bank credit and loans by type, deposits and M2, bank leverage, the prime rate, lending standards from the Senior Loan Officer Survey, and delinquency and charge-off rates.

Financial Conditions and Stress

The Chicago Fed NFCI and its subindices, financial stress indices, the VIX, corporate bond spreads, the excess bond premium and the term spread.
