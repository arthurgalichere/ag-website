Financial, Credit and Business Indicators

The financial and business side of the US economy: how stocks are priced, how firms enter, grow and exit, how much households, businesses and governments borrow, how banks lend, and how tight financial conditions are. The stock market comes first, with its main indicators and its cycles, then business dynamics: how many firms start and exit, and how many jobs they create and destroy. Debt, banks and financial conditions follow. Cycles are measured as on the Real Business Cycles page.

Pages

Main Stock Market Indicators

The S&P 500 from Robert J. Shiller’s data and the dividends of all US corporations: prices, dividends and earnings with their trends, the price–dividend, price–earnings and CAPE ratios, returns, and growth rates, in real or nominal terms.

Stock Market Cycles

How stock prices, dividends, earnings and valuation ratios move over the business cycle: their volatility and co-movement with output, leads and lags, and correlations with the variables of the Real Business Cycles page.

Investor Sentiment and Equity Issuance

The Baker–Wurgler investor sentiment index and its components (IPOs, first-day returns, the closed-end fund discount, the dividend premium, the equity share of new issues), and new equity issues and buybacks relative to the market value of corporate equities, with statistics for calibration.

Business Dynamics

Firm startups and exits, establishment openings and closings, and job creation and destruction in the United States: the Census Bureau’s annual Business Dynamics Statistics since 1978 and the BLS quarterly Business Employment Dynamics since 1992, with statistics for calibration and their cycles.
