Real Business Cycles

This page shows the cyclical component of real GDP: how far output sits above or below its trend in each quarter. The trend is not observed, so it is estimated with three standard filters that can be compared. Shaded bands mark recessions.

Methods

What the cycle measures

Each filter splits 100 × the natural logarithm of quarterly real GDP into a trend and a cyclical component. Because the series is in logs, a cycle value of 2 means output is roughly 2% above its trend; the exact deviation is 100 × (ec/100 − 1), which differs little for values of this size.

The cycle is a statistical estimate, not an observed quantity. Different filters define the trend differently, so their cycles differ in size and timing. Comparing them shows which features are robust to the choice of method.

Hodrick–Prescott (HP) filter

The HP trend is the smooth series that best fits the data while penalising changes in its growth rate. The smoothing parameter is λ = 1600, the convention for quarterly data. The filter is two-sided: each trend value uses past and future observations.

At the end of the sample there are no future observations, so the latest trend and cycle values are the least reliable and are revised as new data arrive.

Hamilton filter

Hamilton (2018) regresses log GDP on its four most recent values known two years earlier (h = 8, p = 4 for quarterly data). The cycle is the part of output that could not be predicted from that information.

The method uses only past data, so it is not revised at the end of the sample in the same way as the HP filter. The first 11 quarters of the sample have no value by construction, and its cycles are usually larger than HP cycles because they include two years of unpredictable changes.

Christiano–Fitzgerald (CF) band-pass filter

The CF filter keeps the fluctuations that repeat every 6 to 32 quarters (1.5 to 8 years), the usual definition of business-cycle frequencies, and removes slower movements and shorter-lived noise. This page uses the asymmetric full-sample version, which assumes log GDP behaves like a random walk with drift and keeps every quarter of the sample.

Like the HP filter, its most recent values rely on fewer observations and are revised as new data arrive.

Recession shading and data revisions

Shaded bands show US recessions from the NBER-based quarterly recession indicator published on FRED (USRECQ). Each band covers the quarters the indicator marks as recession.

Real GDP is revised by the BEA after its first release. Each update of this page uses the latest available history, so earlier values can change.
