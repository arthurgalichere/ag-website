Uncertainty, the Euler Equation, and Precautionary Saving

This interactive example separates the immediate effect of a mean-preserving income risk on expected marginal utility from the household’s equilibrium saving response.

1. Quick Model Overview

Household problem

Maximisation problem

\[ \max_A\; u(c_1)+\beta\,\mathbb{E}[u(c_2)] \] \[ c_1=W_1-A, \qquad c_2=(1+r)A+W_2. \]

The household chooses risk-free saving \(A\) in period 1, before period-2 income is realised.

Resource constraint

Intertemporal budget constraint

\[ c_1+\frac{c_2^s}{1+r} =W_1+\frac{W_2^s}{1+r}, \qquad s\in\{L,H\}. \]

The present value of consumption equals the present value of resources in each possible income state.

Optimality condition

Euler equation

\[ u'(c_1)=\beta(1+r)\,\mathbb{E}[u'(c_2)]. \]

Current marginal utility equals the discounted expected return-weighted marginal utility of future consumption.

Mean-preserving income risk

\[ W_2= \begin{cases} W-\varepsilon & \text{with probability }1/2,\\ W+\varepsilon & \text{with probability }1/2, \end{cases} \qquad \mathbb{E}[W_2]=W. \] \[ c_2^L=(1+r)A+W-\varepsilon, \qquad c_2^H=(1+r)A+W+\varepsilon, \] \[ \mathbb{E}[c_2]=(1+r)A+W, \qquad c_2^L=\mathbb{E}[c_2]-\varepsilon, \qquad c_2^H=\mathbb{E}[c_2]+\varepsilon. \]

Increasing \(\varepsilon\) raises the dispersion of future income and consumption without changing their expected value before saving adjusts.

2. CRRA Utility and the Two Euler Equations

CRRA utility

\[ u(c)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{c^{1-\gamma}-1}{1-\gamma}, & \gamma\neq1,\\[1ex] \ln(c), & \gamma=1, \end{cases} \qquad \gamma>0. \]

Marginal utility

\[ u'(c)=c^{-\gamma}. \]

For the interactive exercise, \(\beta(1+r)=1\). The relevant Euler equation therefore depends on whether \(\varepsilon\) is zero or positive.

Certainty case Active when \(\varepsilon=0\)
\[ c_1^{-\gamma}=\mathbb{E}[c_2]^{-\gamma} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_1=\mathbb{E}[c_2]. \]
Uncertainty case Inactive when \(\varepsilon=0\)
\[ c_1^{-\gamma} =\frac12\left(\mathbb{E}[c_2]-\varepsilon\right)^{-\gamma} +\frac12\left(\mathbb{E}[c_2]+\varepsilon\right)^{-\gamma}. \]
Jensen’s inequality: when \(\varepsilon>0\), convex marginal utility implies \[ \mathbb{E}[u'(c_2)]>u'(\mathbb{E}[c_2]). \] Expected marginal utility rises, so the household reduces \(c_1\) and increases saving until the uncertain Euler equation holds again.

Step 1

Risk before saving adjusts

Hold savings at its certainty level. The low and high consumption states spread around unchanged expected consumption. Convex marginal utility makes \(\mathbb{E}[u'(c_2)]\) rise above \(u'(\mathbb{E}[c_2])\).

Step 2

Equilibrium after saving adjusts

The household lowers \(c_1\) and raises \(A\). This shifts both \(c_2^L\) and \(c_2^H\) to the right until current marginal utility equals expected future marginal utility.
