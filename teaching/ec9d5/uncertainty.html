Uncertainty, the Euler Equation, and Precautionary Saving

This interactive example separates the immediate effect of a mean-preserving income risk on expected marginal utility from the household’s equilibrium saving response.

1. Quick Model Overview

Household problem

Expected-utility maximisation

\[ \max_{A}\; u(c_1)+\beta\,\mathbb{E}\left[u(c_2^s)\right] \]

The household chooses risk-free saving \(A\) in period 1, before period-2 income \(W_2^s\) is realised.

Constraints

Budget constraints

\[ c_1=W_1-A, \qquad c_2^s=(1+r)A+W_2^s, \] \[ c_1+\frac{c_2^s}{1+r}=W_1+\frac{W_2^s}{1+r}, \qquad s\in\{L,H\}. \]

Consumption in each period, and its present value in each possible income state, must match the household’s resources.

Optimality condition

Euler equation

\[ -u'(c_1)+\beta(1+r)\,\mathbb{E}\left[u'(c_2^s)\right]=0 \] \[ \Longrightarrow\quad u'(c_1)=\beta(1+r)\,\mathbb{E}\left[u'(c_2^s)\right]. \]

The marginal utility cost of saving one more unit today equals its discounted expected marginal utility gain tomorrow.

Mean-preserving income risk

\[ W_2^s= \begin{cases} W-\varepsilon & \text{with probability }1/2 \quad (s=L),\\ W+\varepsilon & \text{with probability }1/2 \quad (s=H), \end{cases} \qquad \mathbb{E}[W_2^s]=W. \] \[ \mathbb{E}[c_2^s]=(1+r)A+W, \qquad c_2^L=\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]-\varepsilon, \qquad c_2^H=\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]+\varepsilon. \]

Increasing \(\varepsilon\) spreads future income and consumption without changing their expected value, as long as saving is unchanged.

2. CRRA Utility and the Two Euler Equations

CRRA utility

\[ u(c)= \begin{cases} \dfrac{c^{1-\gamma}-1}{1-\gamma}, & \gamma\neq1,\\[1ex] \ln(c), & \gamma=1, \end{cases} \qquad \gamma>0. \]

Marginal utility

\[ u'(c)=c^{-\gamma}, \qquad u''(c)=-\gamma c^{-\gamma-1}<0, \qquad u'''(c)=\gamma(\gamma+1)c^{-\gamma-2}>0. \]

Marginal utility is decreasing and convex. The larger \(\gamma\), the more curved it is.

For the interactive exercise, \(\beta(1+r)=1\). Which Euler equation applies depends on whether \(\varepsilon\) is zero or positive.

Certainty case Active because ε = 0
\[ c_1^{-\gamma}=\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]^{-\gamma} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_1=\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]. \]
Uncertainty case Inactive because ε = 0
\[ c_1^{-\gamma} =\tfrac12\left(\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]-\varepsilon\right)^{-\gamma} +\tfrac12\left(\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]+\varepsilon\right)^{-\gamma}. \]
Jensen’s inequality: when \(\varepsilon>0\), convex marginal utility implies \[ \mathbb{E}\left[u'(c_2^s)\right]>u'\!\left(\mathbb{E}[c_2^s]\right). \] Expected marginal utility rises, so the household reduces \(c_1\) and increases saving until the uncertain Euler equation holds again. This is precautionary saving. It requires \(u'''>0\) (prudence), which CRRA utility always satisfies.

Step 1

Risk before saving adjusts

Saving stays at its certainty level. The low and high states spread around unchanged expected consumption, and convex marginal utility pushes \(\mathbb{E}[u'(c_2^s)]\) above \(u'(\mathbb{E}[c_2^s])\). The Euler equation would then require a lower \(c_1\).

Step 2

Equilibrium after saving adjusts

The household lowers \(c_1\) and raises \(A\). Both \(c_2^L\) and \(c_2^H\) shift to the right until current marginal utility equals expected future marginal utility.
